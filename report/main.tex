\title{CUDA Kernel Library\\[4pt]
\large Driving a GEMM from naive to compute bound on an RTX 5070}

\begin{abstract}
This report documents a CUDA kernel library whose general matrix multiply path is
driven, through explicit optimization steps and Nsight Compute diagnostic rounds,
from a naive baseline to a compute bound tensor core kernel. Every performance
figure is my kernel measured against the vendor library (cuBLAS, cuSPARSE,
cuFFT, or CUB) on one GPU, an NVIDIA RTX 5070, in the same process. There are no
cross device comparisons. The result that shaped the work is a negative one:
shared memory tiling, the first optimization every course teaches, is 17 percent
slower than the naive kernel it replaces on this cache rich part, and Section
\ref{sec:negative} reports that as a finding in its own right. The top FP16
kernel passes the compute bound gate and reaches 89.9 percent of cuBLAS at 4096
cubed and 88.9 percent at 8192 cubed, with a geometric mean of 79.9 percent over
the four swept shapes. Alongside the GEMM ladder the library carries GEMV, TRSM,
CSR SpMV, FFT and convolution, reduction and scan, and a cuSOLVER wrapper, all
verified against the vendor library, with a roofline whose compute ceilings come
from the hardware rather than from a vendor library. The performance figures in
this build of the report were collected before the benchmark clocks were locked
and before two timed region defects were fixed; the corrections register in
\texttt{docs/CORRECTIONS.md} lists what that affects, Section \ref{sec:repro}
says which numbers are v1 era, and every number awaiting the 1.1.0 sweep reads
\emph{pending} rather than being estimated.
\end{abstract}
