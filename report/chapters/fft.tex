\section{FFT and convolution}
\label{sec:fft}

The FFT family is new in 1.1.0. Its mechanisms are implemented and tested, and the
locked clock sweep measured them. The result is a clean loss to cuFFT with a fully
diagnosed cause, which is more useful than a narrow win would have been: the best
hand rung reaches 60.5 percent of cuFFT at $2^{20}$ and 38.3 percent at $2^{24}$,
against a gate that asked for 70. Section \ref{sec:fftgate} below is the
diagnosis.

\subsection{Stockham, and why there is no bit reversal pass}

The transform is Stockham autosort~\cite{vanloan1992}. Each stage writes to a
second buffer with the digit permutation folded into the output address, so every
stage reads and writes contiguous runs. At stride $p$ a thread owns one butterfly:
it reads elements $j$ and $j + n/2$ of the source, twists the second by
$\exp(-2\pi i k / 2p)$ with $k = j \bmod p$, and writes the sum and the difference
to their permuted destinations. The radix 4 and radix 8 stages are the same
statement with $R$ in place of 2, and there is exactly one implementation of each
in the tree, called from two translation units, because a radix 8 DFT written
twice is a radix 8 DFT that will eventually disagree with itself.

The in place alternative needs a separate bit reversal pass whose access pattern is
a scatter across the whole array, one sector per element at large $n$. On a family
that is already memory bound that pass costs a full memory round trip, which is a
whole extra pass out of the five the four step form needs. The price of avoiding it
is the second buffer, and the shared memory budget pays it. Cooley-Tukey with a bit
reversal pass is not implemented here and is not planned.

\subsection{The four step form}

For $n$ above the shared resident bound the plan factors $n = n_1 n_2$ with both
factors at or below $2^{12}$ and runs a transpose, batched size $n_2$ transforms
with the cross twiddle folded into the store epilogue, a transpose, batched size
$n_1$ transforms, and a final transpose that puts the output where the caller wants
it. Five passes, each one read and one write, so $5 \times 16n$ bytes; against
$\log_2 n$ passes for the naive multi kernel rung that is 5 against 24 at
$n = 2^{24}$. The cross twiddle costs no pass of its own because it rides on an
epilogue, and neither does a fused convolution's pointwise multiply.

The factorization splits $\log_2 n$ as evenly as it can, larger half first, which
keeps both factors inside the shared resident bound for every $n$ up to $2^{24}$
and gives both batched passes the most blocks to fill 48 SMs with. An override
exists and is validated rather than trusted.

\subsection{Convolution, and the crossover}

The headline figure of this family is a crossover, not a percentage. The FFT
path's cost barely moves with the filter length $M$; the direct path is $2NM$ FLOP
and does not stop growing. Four variants are implemented: an FFT path with a
standalone pointwise multiply kernel, an FFT path with the filter spectrum multiply
folded into a store epilogue (the same epilogue fusion argument the GEMM bias study
uses, and the same one FlashAttention makes at a larger scale~\cite{dao2022}), a
tiled time domain kernel, and the same tiling with the taps in constant memory.

Two implementation notes are worth keeping. The obvious direct tiling, a signal
tile plus a halo of $M-1$ samples, does not survive the top of the sweep:
$M = 16384$ is a 64 KB halo before the tile is counted. Chunking the filter instead
holds the shared footprint at about 3 KB whatever $M$ is. And the constant memory
bank is one per module rather than one per plan, so a plan binds its taps once and
records that it owns them; a call from a plan that does not own the bank rebinds
first, and a direct call from an owner that never bound throws rather than
convolving with somebody else's filter.

crossing read out of the data by log interpolation and printed into the caption. It
is not drawn by hand, and when the summary holds no convolution rows the generator
removes the figure and writes the paragraph below instead of rebuilding this
chapter around a stale picture.

\paragraph{Why the convolution percent is a time ratio.} This one nearly shipped
as a wrong number, so it is worth writing down. Every other percent in this report
is a ratio of throughputs, which is safe when both sides count FLOP the same way.
The convolution rungs do not: a direct time domain rung does $2NM$ FLOP and an FFT
rung does about $5N\log N$ for the same answer, so dividing one rung's GFLOP/s by
cuFFT's compares two models rather than two speeds. On the committed rows that
does not merely blur the comparison, it inverts it. At $M = 8$ the direct constant
rung runs in 0.0814 ms against cuFFT's 1.8603 ms, twenty three times faster, and
the throughput ratio reads 77.5 percent, as though it had lost. At $M = 4096$ it
runs in 9.1418 ms against 1.8588 ms, five times slower, and the throughput ratio
reads 352.9 percent, as though it had won by a wide margin. Both kernels compute
the same convolution, so the honest comparison is time, and the generator computes
these four cells from the medians against the cuFFT row at the same $N$ and $M$.

\subsection{The gate, and why the ladder loses}
\label{sec:fftgate}

Two of the family's gate clauses failed, and round 16 measured the cause of each
rather than leaving them as shrugs.

\paragraph{The traffic model has no L2 term.} The clause asks that measured
\texttt{dram\_\_bytes.sum} land within 15 percent of the model each row declares.
At $2^{24}$ it does: 1977.03 MB measured against a declared 2,147,483,648, which is
3.5 percent low. At $2^{20}$ it fails badly, 64.08 MB against 117,440,512 declared,
45.4 percent low, and the reason is that the ping pong buffers are 8 MB each at
that length, so the whole working set sits inside the 48 MB L2 and six of the seven
stage boundaries never reach DRAM. The model assumes all seven do. This is the
clause doing its job rather than a defect in the measurement, and it has a
consequence worth stating plainly: the effective GB/s of that row reads 1091.942,
above the 579 GB/s DRAM roof of this part, and it is above the roof because 45
percent of the bytes in its numerator were served by L2. That figure is therefore
not usable as evidence of anything, and no percent of roof is quoted from it.

\paragraph{The rungs trail cuFFT on passes, not on efficiency.} At $2^{24}$ the
radix 8 rung is not running badly. Each of its eight stages runs at 83.47 percent
DRAM Throughput and 552.35 GB/s against 10.06 percent Compute (SM) Throughput, so
it is at about 95 percent of the measured DRAM roof on every pass it makes. It
simply makes eight passes where cuFFT makes three: 1977.03 MB against 744.37 MB, a
factor of 2.66, and the committed medians are 3.721280 ms against 1.426944 ms, a
factor of 2.61. Those two ratios agreeing to within 2 percent is the whole
diagnosis. Radix 8 is the wrong lever, because it reduces stages inside a pass and
the cost is the number of passes. The four step form and a larger resident
transform are the levers that move that number, and they are the queued work.

One prediction made earlier in this chapter was wrong and is corrected rather than
deleted. The expected limiter on the resident rung was a stride $2^s$ bank conflict
in the butterfly exchange. The rung measures \emph{zero} conflicts against 319,488
shared load wavefronts. The real constraint is occupancy: 65.54 KB of dynamic
shared memory per block gives one block per SM and 16.65 percent achieved
occupancy, at 34.04 percent Memory Throughput and 26.41 percent Compute SOL, so it
is latency bound with nothing to hide the latency behind.
